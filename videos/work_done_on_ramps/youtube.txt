TITLE
Work Done on Ramps: Against Friction and Gravity | A Level Further Mechanics


DESCRIPTION
When you pull a box up a rough slope, the pulling force does two jobs: it lifts the box against gravity and it overcomes friction. This animated lesson shows where each part of Td = μRd + mgh comes from. It's for A level Further Maths (Further Mechanics 1).

Full lesson and worksheet: https://mr-mathematics.com

In this video you will:
• resolve the weight along the slope to show that the work done against gravity is mg sin α × d, which is mgh
• see why only the vertical height matters, not the route the box takes
• find the friction from R = mg cos α and show that the work done against friction is μR × d
• combine the two to get Td = μRd + mgh for a box pulled at a constant speed
• follow four steps for any ramp problem, work through an example, then try a question yourself

The example: a 6 kg box pulled 20 m up a rough plane inclined at α, where tan α = 8/15 and μ = 0.4. Find the work done against friction and against gravity.
Your turn: a 1.5 kg parcel pulled 4 m up a rough surface inclined at arctan 3/4, with 50 J of work done. Find the coefficient of friction.
