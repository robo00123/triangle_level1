TITLE
Introducing Work Done: Force, Gravity and Friction | A Level Further Mechanics


DESCRIPTION
What is work done, and how do you calculate it? This animated lesson shows where W = Fs comes from, then uses it to find the work done against gravity and against friction. It's for A level Further Maths (Further Mechanics 1).

Full lesson and worksheet: https://mr-mathematics.com

In this video you will:
• see why a force does work only when it moves an object
• watch the work double when the force or the distance doubles, which gives W = Fs
• lift a box at a steady speed to show that the work done against gravity is mgh
• push a crate across a rough floor to show that the work done against friction is μmg × s
• work through four examples, then try a question yourself

The examples:
1. A crate pushed 12 m by a constant force of 25 N
2. A 12 kg box lifted vertically through 5 m
3. Finding the height of a tower from 24 kJ of work done on a 60 kg crate
4. Finding the coefficient of friction for an 8 kg crate pushed 6 m
Your turn: the work done against friction on a 5 kg crate moving at 4 m s⁻¹ for 3 seconds
