A crate of mass 5 kilograms is pushed across a rough horizontal floor, at a constant speed of 4 metres per second.

Right, let's go through it.

First, the distance. At 4 metres per second for 3 seconds, the crate travels 4 times 3, which is 12 metres.

Next, the friction. On a horizontal floor, R equals m g, so the friction is 0.4 times 5 times 9.8. That's 19.6 newtons.

The work done against friction is the friction times the distance. 19.6 times 12 is 235.2, so the work done is 235.2 joules.

A common mistake is to multiply the friction by the time instead of the distance. That gives 58.8, which isn't work done at all. Work done is always force times distance.
