DESCRIPTION
Why is u · w = v · w and −e u · I = v · I when a particle rebounds off a smooth wall that isn't in the i or j direction? This animated lesson proves both results with the scalar product, then uses them to find a rebound velocity without working out a single angle. It's for A level Further Maths (Further Mechanics 1).

Full lesson and worksheet: https://mr-mathematics.com

In this video you will:
• split the velocities along and perpendicular to the wall, using a smooth wall and Newton's law of restitution
• see the scalar product as the shadow of one vector on another, and why it turns negative past 90°
• slide u tail to tail with ŵ to prove u · w = v · w
• use the unit circle to show cos(90° + α) = −sin α, then prove −e u · I = v · I
• apply both results to a sphere hitting a wall parallel to 3i + 4j

The example: a smooth sphere moving with velocity (11i − 2j) m s⁻¹ hits a smooth vertical wall parallel to 3i + 4j. The coefficient of restitution is ½. Find the velocity of the sphere after the impact.
