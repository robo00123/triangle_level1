Work done

The work done by a constant force is the magnitude of the force multiplied by the distance moved in the direction of the force.

Work done = force × distance

Work done is measured in joules (J).

Work done against gravity on a slope

When a particle of mass m kg moves a distance d m up a line of greatest slope, it rises a vertical height h = d sin α.

The component of its weight acting down the slope is mg sin α.

Work done against gravity = mgh

Only the vertical height gained matters, not the distance moved along the slope.

Take g = 9.8 m s−2 and give answers to 3 significant figures.

Show that the work done against gravity is mgh

Resolve the weight parallel to the slope.

Component of the weight down the slope = mg sin α

Work done against gravity = force × distance = mg sin α × d

From the triangle, sin α = [[h/d]] so h = d sin α

Work done against gravity = mg(d sin α) = mgh

Work done against friction on a rough slope

Resolving perpendicular to the plane gives R = mg cos α.

When the particle is moving, friction is limiting, so F = μR. Friction acts down the slope when the particle moves up it.

Work done against friction = μR × d

Moving at a constant speed means there is no acceleration, so the forces parallel to the slope balance.

When solving problems on a ramp, always:

  • Draw a force diagram showing R, F, the weight and any pulling force.
  • Use a right-angled triangle to find exact values of sin α and cos α.
  • Resolve perpendicular to the plane to find R.
  • Use F = μR when the particle is moving.
  • Use the vertical height h for the work done against gravity.
Show that the work done by T in pulling the particle a distance d up the slope at a constant speed is mgd(sin α + μ cos α)

Resolve perpendicular to the slope: R = mg cos α

Friction is limiting: F = μR = μmg cos α

Resolve parallel to the slope (constant speed, so the forces balance):

T = F + mg sin α = mg(sin α + μ cos α)

Work done by T = T × d = mgd(sin α + μ cos α)

1. A rough plane is inclined at an angle α to the horizontal, where tan α = [[8/15]].

A box of mass 6 kg is pulled at a constant speed up a line of greatest slope of the plane.

The coefficient of friction between the box and the plane is 0.4.

a) Calculate the magnitude of the frictional force acting on the box.

The box moves a distance of 20 m up the plane. Calculate:

b) the work done against friction

c) the work done against gravity.

tan α = [[8/15]], so use an 8, 15, 17 triangle:

sin α = [[8/17]] and cos α = [[15/17]]

Resolve perpendicular to the plane:

R = 6g cos α = 6 × 9.8 × [[15/17]] = 51.88… N

a) The box is moving, so friction is limiting.

F = μR = 0.4 × 51.88… = 20.8 N (3 s.f.)

b) Work done against friction = F × d = 20.75… × 20 = 415 J (3 s.f.)

c) Vertical height: h = 20 sin α = 20 × [[8/17]] = 9.411… m

Work done against gravity = mgh = 6 × 9.8 × 9.411… = 553 J (3 s.f.)

2. A rough surface is inclined at an angle arctan [[3/4]] to the horizontal.

A parcel of mass 1.5 kg is pulled 4 m at a constant speed up the surface by a force acting along a line of greatest slope.

The only resistances to the motion are those due to friction and gravity.

The work done by the force is 50 J.

Calculate the coefficient of friction between the parcel and the surface.

tan α = [[3/4]], so use a 3, 4, 5 triangle:

sin α = [[3/5]] and cos α = [[4/5]]

Height gained: h = 4 sin α = 4 × [[3/5]] = 2.4 m

Work done against gravity = mgh = 1.5 × 9.8 × 2.4 = 35.28 J

The work done by the force is used against gravity and friction:

50 = 35.28 + work done against friction

Work done against friction = 14.72 J

Resolve perpendicular: R = 1.5g cos α = 1.5 × 9.8 × [[4/5]] = 11.76 N

Work done against friction = μR × d, so 14.72 = μ × 11.76 × 4

μ = [[14.72/47.04]] = 0.313 (3 s.f.)

3. A rough surface is inclined at 25° to the horizontal.

A sledge of mass 4 kg is pulled at a constant speed up the surface by a force P acting along a line of greatest slope.

The coefficient of friction between the sledge and the surface is 0.15.

Modelling the sledge as a particle, calculate the work done by P when the sledge travels 12 m.

Resolve perpendicular to the plane:

R = 4g cos 25° = 4 × 9.8 × cos 25° = 35.52… N

The sledge is moving, so friction is limiting:

F = μR = 0.15 × 35.52… = 5.329… N

Resolve parallel to the plane (constant speed, so the forces balance):

P = F + 4g sin 25° = 5.329… + 16.56… = 21.89… N

Work done by P = P × d = 21.89… × 12 = 263 J (3 s.f.)

Check: work done against friction + work done against gravity

= 5.329… × 12 + 4 × 9.8 × 12 sin 25° = 63.94… + 198.7… = 263 J ✓

Work done by a force at an angle

Only the component of a force in the direction of motion does work.

A force of magnitude P N acting at an angle θ to the direction of motion has a component P cos θ in the direction of motion and a component P sin θ perpendicular to it.

Work done = P cos θ × d

The perpendicular component does no work because the particle does not move in that direction. For the same reason, the normal reaction R does no work.

In Examples 1 to 3 the pulling force acted along the slope, so θ = 0° and the work done was simply force × distance.

When a force is inclined to the direction of motion, always:

  • Mark the angle between the force and the direction of motion on your diagram.
  • Resolve the force parallel to the direction of motion.
  • Multiply this component by the distance moved.
Show that the work done by a force P, inclined at θ to the horizontal, in pulling a particle a distance d across a horizontal surface is Pd cos θ

Resolve the force horizontally and vertically.

Horizontal component = P cos θ, vertical component = P sin θ

The particle moves horizontally, so only P cos θ does work.

Work done = horizontal component × distance moved

= P cos θ × d = Pd cos θ

4. A crate is dragged 18 m across a smooth horizontal floor by a rope.

The tension in the rope is 32 N and the rope is inclined at 20° to the horizontal.

By modelling the crate as a particle, calculate the work done by the tension.

The crate moves horizontally, so only the horizontal component of the tension does work.

Horizontal component = 32 cos 20° = 30.07… N

Work done = horizontal component of force × distance moved

= 32 cos 20° × 18

= 541.2…

The work done by the tension is 541 J (3 s.f.)

The vertical component, 32 sin 20°, is perpendicular to the motion, so it does no work.
